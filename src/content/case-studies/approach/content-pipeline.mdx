### The chain

Topic research, keyword discovery, SEO keyword analysis, drafting, internal linking, image generation,
image classification and processing, publishing.

Each stage hands structured output to the next rather than free text. That is not a stylistic
preference: it means a failure is attributable to a stage instead of surfacing as a bad article
at the end with no way to tell where it went wrong.

### The part that is not the model

A reader in 2026 has seen a lot of AI content pipelines and assumes the worst of them. Fair
enough. So this section has to be specific rather than reassuring.

Internal linking is the clearest case. Ask a model to add internal links and it produces links
that look entirely correct and point at nothing, because it is predicting plausible URLs rather
than consulting the site. The pipeline instead maintains its own index of pages that exist and
links against that. A link either resolves or it is not inserted. There is no third option where
it is probably fine.

The same principle runs through the rest. The model drafts; deterministic checks decide what
ships. Anything that would embarrass a client if it were wrong is not left to a probability.

### The alternative I rejected

One prompt, one article, straight to the CMS. It is the fastest thing to build and it is what
the demo of this always looks like.

Rejected because the output is unusable at the point it matters. Generic copy that could be
about any business does not sell anything, and links that resolve to nothing are worse than no
links, because they damage the site they were meant to help. Almost all the value here is in the
stages either side of generation, which is exactly the part the one-prompt version skips.

### Choosing what to write about

This is the stage most content pipelines skip, and skipping it is why so much of their output
reads as filler. Ask a model for article ideas about a store and it returns ten titles that would
suit any store selling anything in that category.

So topic selection is constrained by things outside the model: questions customers actually ask,
questions the products themselves raise, comparisons between items the store genuinely stocks,
and subjects with commercial intent rather than subjects that merely sound relevant. The keyword
analysis narrows that further to what people search for.

The effect is that an article has a reason to exist before a word of it is written, and the
internal linking stage has somewhere real to point, because the subject was drawn from the
catalogue in the first place.

### Images are checked, not just generated

Generated imagery is classified and processed before it is attached rather than dropped in as it
comes back. An image that does not match the article is the fastest way for a reader to work out
that nobody looked at the page, and it undoes the credibility of text that may be perfectly good.

### Publishing into two systems

Shopify and WordPress disagree about what a page is. Rather than fork the pipeline, publishing is
the only stage that knows which target it is writing to, and everything upstream produces the
same structured output regardless. Adding a client on a platform already supported does not mean
writing a new pipeline.
