### The shape of the pipeline

Orders arrive from two sources, are validated, are assigned a carton, are rated across
carriers, and come out the other end as a label. Each stage can fail independently, so each
stage has to be able to hand a specific problem back to a person rather than failing the whole
run.

### Why the carton is the interesting decision

Carriers do not bill on weight alone. They bill on dimensional weight — a function of the
box's volume — and charge the greater of that and the actual weight. So an item that weighs
very little in a box that is slightly too large can cost the same as something several times
heavier.

That makes carton selection the step where the money is. It has to satisfy several things at
once: the items must physically fit, the box has to be one the warehouse actually stocks, the
dimensions must stay under the carrier's oversize thresholds, and among the boxes that qualify
it should pick the one that rates cheapest for the destination.
